## What everyone shares (the category cliches)
1. Centered hero, gradient or tinted background, a pill CTA, a star or user count line.
2. Tech roles in the sample cards (Datadog, Software Engineer). Our seeker is a call center agent, a data entry clerk, an admin assistant. Nobody shows *their* jobs.
3. "AI" as the headline. Our ICP is scam wary and has been burned by ghost jobs and auto renew trials. AI language reads as more risk.
4. Italic emphasis, glowing gradients, rounded 16px cards with an icon in a circle.
5. Price hidden or deferred. DirectRoles shows no price above the fold.
6. Apply is a button. Nobody makes the act of going to the employer feel physical or honest.

## What this ICP trusts
Plain words, a visible price, real dates, a refund promise, the employer's own domain printed on the page. Think of the paper notice in a shop window: it is local, it is dated, it names the employer, you can tear off the number.

## The gap we own
Be the only one that looks like a **notice board of real, dated jobs** with the **price on the first screen**, in a calm green and warm paper palette that is not indigo, not mint, not blue. Show *ordinary* roles with real looking dates and "posted 2 days ago, never reposted". Competitors sell AI and speed; we sell honesty and a receipt.

Differs on at least four axes from the closest three (DirectRoles, Jobright, Simplify): palette (teal on paper, no gradients), type (Bricolage + Public Sans, no Inter/Titillium/DM Serif), layout (left aligned, 2 pane reader, tear off card), imagery (real notices and source hosts, no stock, no dashboards), tone (accountable, no hype).
